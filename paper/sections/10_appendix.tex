\section{MemConflict audit}
\label{app:memconflict}

\paragraph{Procedure.}
Every evaluated question was audited blind to all model outputs: a rater saw only the question, the gold answer, the construction record and the evidence sessions (user turns in full, assistant turns truncated), and had to support each verdict with verbatim dialogue quotes, which we checked against the dialogue. A question is \emph{clean} if the user's own statements support the gold as worded, the value is about the user, and, for static items, the later statement genuinely contradicts the earlier one; \emph{ambiguous} if a careful reader could defend another answer or the planted conflict is not a real contradiction; a \emph{label error} if the gold is unsupported or belongs to someone else. The rubric is reproduced in Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}. Four raters audited every question independently: R1 and R2 on Claude models, R3 on a GPT model (Codex CLI) and R4 on a Gemini model (Antigravity CLI), the last two in sandboxes with no file or network access beyond the question packets. No rater saw another's verdicts, and each set of verdicts was frozen (and, for R3 and R4, hashed) before being joined with the others or with any model output. Table~\ref{tab:memconflict_audit} gives the counts and Table~\ref{tab:memconflict_dropped} lists the questions excluded from the main clean set.

\paragraph{Findings.}
R1 and R2 agree on \nm{memconflict_audit90_r2/agreement_binary_pct}\% of questions (Cohen's $\kappa=\nm{memconflict_audit90_r2/kappa_binary}$); pairwise $\kappa$ over all four raters ranges from \nm{tab8/kappa_pairs/R2_R4} to \nm{tab8/kappa_pairs/R3_R4} (the two non-Claude raters agree most) and Fleiss' $\kappa$ is \nm{tab8/kappa_fleiss4}. The new raters are stricter: of the \nm{memconflict_audit90_r2/both_clean} questions R1 and R2 both call clean, \nm{tab8/all/evaluated/clean_all4} are clean for all four. Outright label errors are rare; most flagged items are static conflicts whose later statement does not logically contradict the earlier one (a lower degree does not refute a higher one as ``highest degree'', records listing a mother as unknown do not refute having one) or is voiced only by the assistant. The pre-set gate of \nm{memconflict_audit90/gate_pct}\% clean fails for every rater, and we report it as failed.

By type, \nm{tab8/dynamic/evaluated/clean_both} of \nm{tab8/dynamic/evaluated/n} dynamic, \nm{tab8/static/evaluated/clean_both} of \nm{tab8/static/evaluated/n} static and \nm{tab8/conditional/evaluated/clean_both} of \nm{tab8/conditional/evaluated/n} conditional questions are clean for R1 and R2 (Table~\ref{tab:memconflict_audit}); the clean re-scoring in Table~\ref{tab:memconflict_clean} keeps these \nm{memconflict_audit90_r2/both_clean} questions. On the stricter set of \nm{tab8/all/evaluated/clean_all4} questions all four raters call clean, the static comparison keeps its direction but is not significant under the GPT-family labels (Section~\ref{sec:memconflict}).

\paragraph{Static answers relabelled.}
To check the judge's static rubric, a GPT-family model labelled the answers of B1 and DeltaWAL-Versioned (lag 0) on the clean static questions (\nm{paired/mc_static_codex_fb_clean70/pooled/n} per system, over three readers) as giving the true value, the false value, both, or neither, blind to which system produced it. Under these labels DeltaWAL-Versioned believes the false statement more often by \nm{paired/mc_static_codex_fb_clean70/pooled/diff_pp} points (permutation $p=\nm{paired/mc_static_codex_fb_clean70/pooled/p_perm}$), against \nm{paired/mc_static_falsebelief_dwv0_vs_b1/pooled/diff_pp} under the judge.
